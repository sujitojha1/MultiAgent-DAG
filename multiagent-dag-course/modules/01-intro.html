What This Thing Actually Does

You type a question. A team of AI specialists assembles itself, works in parallel, and hands you back one answer. Let's watch it happen.

Start with something you'd actually type

Imagine you open a terminal and run this:

CODE 
uv run python flow.py "Compare the populations of London, Paris, and Berlin"
PLAIN ENGLISH

You ask one plain-English question.

Behind the scenes, that single line kicks off a whole team of AI workers — not one big AI doing everything.

This project is an AI agent. Its one job: take a messy human request and figure out how to answer it by orchestrating a team of small, focused specialists.

The metaphor: a newsroom on deadline

The cleanest way to picture this system is a newsroom. You're the reader who wants a story. Nobody writes the whole thing alone:

📋
The Editor — reads your request and decides who needs to work on it

In the code this is the Planner.

🔎
The Reporters — go out, gather facts, come back

Here, three Researchers — one each for London, Paris, Berlin — working at the same time.

✍️
The Copy Editor — assembles the final piece the reader sees

The Formatter writes the answer that lands in your terminal.

💡
The big idea

Instead of one giant AI trying to do everything, the work is split into small specialists. Each one is good at exactly one thing. Engineers call this separation of concerns — and it's the single most important pattern in this whole codebase.

Where does the team "live"? In a graph.

The team isn't a fixed pipeline. It's drawn as a graph — dots (the specialists) connected by arrows (who waits for whom).

The graph grows as work finishes

That last point is the magic. The Planner doesn't know the final shape up front — the graph adds new nodes while it runs. That's why this is called a growing-graph orchestrator.

🧭
The phrase to remember

"The graph is the agent loop." Older AI agents ran one big think-act-repeat loop. Here, the loop is replaced by a graph of specialists that draws itself as it goes. You'll see exactly how in Module 3.

The very first thing that happens

When you hit enter, the system does almost nothing clever at first. It just puts one sticky note on an empty board:

CODE · flow.py 
store.write_query(query)
graph = Graph()
graph.add_node("planner", inputs=["USER_QUERY"])

Why this matters for steering AI

Everything downstream is decided by the Planner reading your words. If your results are wrong, the first question is always: "What did the Planner think I asked?" You'll learn to inspect exactly that in Module 5.
